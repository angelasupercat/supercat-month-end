Executive Summary

August was a genuine recovery month, on both revenue and cost lines. Total revenue came in at $164,797 (+$11,660 / +7.6% MoM off July's restated $153,138; −$1,871 / -1.1% vs. August budget $166,668) — essentially back on plan. Both subscriptions ($160,847, -0.2% vs. budget) and professional services ($3,950, up from July's depressed $1,450) recovered.

COGS came in at $17,478 — up +3.7% MoM and +15.5% vs. budget — reflecting Mixpanel's annual subscription amortized evenly across Jun–Aug and a modest increase in Rackspace hosting. Gross margin held essentially flat at 89.4% (+0.4pp MoM), still 1.5 points under the 90.9% budget.

OpEx came in at $120,032 (+5.9% MoM, +9.3% vs. budget). Engineering Payroll rose to $20,419 — Bruce's PTO payout, which also lifted 401(k)/benefits costs. Contract Admin Services rose to $34,999 (+$1,700) because Jack Crabtree is no longer a provider. Sales Payroll increased on additional commission paid, and Show/Conference reflects the High Point Market rental. Partially offsetting: Engineering Computer & Software normalized down to $3,894 as Cursor/dev-tool spend eased. Looking to September, adjustments to marketing and SaaS spend are expected to bring OpEx down to roughly $100,000.

Net effect: operating profit came in at $27,287 (+18.8% MoM, −34.5% vs. budget of $41,683) and net income at $5,342 (+367.2% MoM, −74.8% vs. budget of $21,245).

Cash — bank cash fell $142,236 → $129,103 (−$13,133), a burn 72% smaller than July's −$46,902. AR collections came through (AR dropped $40,487 → $13,017), and AP was extended (+$38,322, to $50,836). AmEx was paid down sharply (−$43,792). Cash/MRR compressed further — 0.94× → 0.80×.

Forward look (single-track forecast, no new logos — worst case per instruction): the Master Forecast model (rebased 2026-09-08 from actual August, "New logos per month" = 0) projects September as the first month of positive cash flow without any investor injection — net change in cash of +$10,550 — provided the business collects 90–95% of AR invoiced for the month, alongside annual renewals and expansions landing as scheduled. From there, cash builds to ~$235,779 by December.

Headline: Revenue and COGS both essentially on plan; OpEx — mostly explained by Bruce's PTO payout, the Crabtree provider change, and higher sales commission — is the remaining gap, expected to ease toward $100K in September. Cash burn moderated sharply on stronger collections.
