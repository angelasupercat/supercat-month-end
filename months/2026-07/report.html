Executive Summary

July was a one-off–heavy revenue miss with still-positive operations, then crushed on cash by debt service. Figures below are as reported in Financial results (18).

Total revenue landed at $151,718 (−$16,597 / -9.9% MoM from June’s $168,315; −$15,397 / -9.2% vs July budget $167,115). Roughly $17.1K of that MoM gap is explained by the CopperSmith credit/refund ($11,150) and the Fine Art annual Sales Portal reversal ($5,940) — leaving ~$3.5K of residual MoM softness (mostly subscriptions). Accrual operations still held: gross margin 88.9%, operating profit $23,034, OpEx $111,826 ≈ on budget (−$1,004). After $21,831 interest, monthly net income was $1,203. The gap to plan is top-line / mix, not an expense blowout.

Revenue mix: Subscriptions $150,568 (-1.2% MoM; −$11,047 vs budget). Professional services $1,150 (vs June $15,850 and budget $5,500). Core iPad $88,942 (up vs June $87,029).

Cash remains the binding constraint — and July was a slower collection month. Bank cash $189,138 → $142,236 (−$46,902). Big-ticket invoices (notably Legrand and Group Courschene) were not collected in-month, compounding the bank burn. Prefer bank Δ over Fathom FCF (~−$14,000), which typically excludes loan principal. Full July debt service ~$41,433. Cash / MRR compressed to 0.94×. Deferred revenue fell −$12,275.

Trailing 6-month ARR (average): $1,852,450 (down from $1,879,858).

Expense posture (light): July Sales cooled to $31.4K after a Jan→Jun climb ($38.7K → $44.7K). Do not treat July as the new normal until August holds. Engineering Computer & Software stepped up sharply ($3.6K → $7.9K MoM) — ownership needed; total OpEx remains on plan.

Forward look (primary = Migration + New Logos): cash path recovers through H2 toward ~$215.2K by Dec in that export. Main Forecast is the downside (~$49.1K Dec). Manage to Migration; treat Main as compare.

Headline: One-off–heavy MoM revenue miss on the books; underlying residual softness modest; NI still positive; cash after debt service + slow big-ticket collections is the structural issue; OpEx is not the July villain.
